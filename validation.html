What the benchmark is

Seasonal-naive means the realized daily mean of the same weekday one week earlier — the standard reference in price forecasting, and what a desk without a model falls back on. MASE below 1.0 beats it.

The diagnosis

The error has one name: the German price move.

Denmark clears in the same coupled auction as Germany. When the German price jumps and the model is holding yesterday's level, the miss is the size of the jump.

What the relationship says

Forecast error tracks the unanticipated German price move almost one for one: slope 1.0, with an R² of 0.91 in DK1 and 0.94 in DK2.

When Germany rises, we under-call

Three days, average miss €54. The interconnector carries the German price into both zones even when local wind is strong.

When wind surges, we over-call

Three days, average miss €34. Denmark decouples below Germany and the model keeps the imported level.

When Germany holds, we are right

2 October: the German price moved €5 and our error was under €8 in both zones.

Based on the seven delivery days in this window where the German anchor is recorded in the model's own driver attribution. A small sample, to be re-measured over a longer window before it is quoted as a constant.

What comes next

The remaining work is one forecast.

The constraint, stated plainly

Tomorrow's German price is not available to us. It clears in the same coupled auction, at the same moment, as the Danish price we are trying to predict. Closing the gap means forecasting the German outcome from data published before gate closure.

What is available before gate closure

German wind, solar and load forecasts from the TSOs, published well before noon. Forward and futures quotes for next-day delivery. Interconnector capacity and planned outages. None of it requires reconstructing the market-coupling algorithm.

Why this is worth publishing

A forecast whose error is overwhelmingly attributable to one measurable driver is in better shape than one that is merely closer on average. It tells a customer which days to trust it on — the days the German anchor is holding still — and it tells us exactly what to build next.
